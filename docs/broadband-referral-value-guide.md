# Is a broadband referral reward actually a good deal?

*A practical guide to comparing the whole contract, not just the cashback.*

Published by [marketsearchprojects](https://github.com/marketsearchprojects). Source checks: 7 November 2026.

## Start with two numbers, not one

A referral reward can improve a deal. It cannot make an unsuitable connection suitable, and it does not automatically make a more expensive contract cheaper.

Compare these separately:

1. **The amount you commit to paying:** broadband bills, required extras, setup charges and switching costs.
2. **The possible benefit after a qualifying reward:** the same total, less the reward you actually expect to receive.

Keeping both numbers visible prevents conditional cashback from looking like a guaranteed discount on the monthly bill.

## The calculation

For the same comparison period:

```text
Gross cost =
    all monthly broadband charges in that period
  + required monthly extras
  + setup and delivery charges
  + existing-provider exit charges and unavoidable overlap

Conditional net cost =
    gross cost
  - eligible customer reward
  - separately confirmed reimbursements that can be combined

Monthly equivalent = total cost / comparison months
```

If the monthly price changes during the contract, add each pricing stage separately. Do not multiply the introductory price by every month.

Count a reimbursement only against a cost you also included. For example, adding a £100 exit fee and subtracting its confirmed £100 reimbursement leaves that part of the comparison at zero. Subtracting the reimbursement without adding the fee would invent a saving.

A monthly equivalent is a planning number, **not the amount the provider will bill each month**. Cash-flow timing still matters.

## Worked example: the cheaper headline can lose

These are invented offers to demonstrate the arithmetic, not live prices from YouFibre or any other provider. Both cover 24 months with no scheduled price changes.

| Item | Offer A | Offer B |
| --- | ---: | ---: |
| Broadband monthly price | £28 | £30 |
| Required monthly extra | £3 | £0 |
| Setup and switching costs | £0 | £0 |
| Gross cost over 24 months | £744 | £720 |
| Conditional customer reward | £50 | £0 |
| Net cost if that reward is received | £694 | £720 |
| Monthly equivalent after reward | £28.92 | £30.00 |

Without the reward, A costs **£24 more**, despite its cheaper headline price. If its £50 reward is received, A costs **£26 less**.

That is the decision: is the £26 conditional advantage worth choosing A, given its service, terms and reward requirements? A bigger cashback headline is not a substitute for this calculation.

## Use the full commitment, not an attractive slice

A first-year comparison can be useful for a household budget. It does not show the full liability on a longer minimum term.

For a 24-month offer, calculate the complete 24 months as well. Include any price increases specified in the quote, the cost of required equipment or extras, and an overlap period if you must pay both providers during the switch.

For offers with different minimum terms, show both the total commitment and a common comparison horizon. State any assumed renewal or post-contract price rather than quietly treating it as known.

## A referral eligibility checklist

YouFibre's [published referral terms](https://www.youfibre.com/legal/refer-a-friend-terms-and-conditions/) describe requirements including a new customer, the referral code on the original order, a qualifying 24-month contract, and an installed and active service for at least 30 days with the first full bill paid. They say a code cannot be added retrospectively and restrict combining the scheme with other promotions or partner discounts.

Its [referral information page](https://www.youfibre.com/refer/) also explains that processing a successful reward can take up to 60 days. Check the current terms and your order confirmation rather than relying on a cached offer.

Before ordering, save:

- the address-specific package and minimum term;
- every monthly pricing stage and mandatory extra;
- confirmation that the referral was recorded;
- the customer reward amount and qualifying conditions;
- any separate exit-fee reimbursement and whether it can be combined;
- the expected reward process and the appropriate support route.

Never budget using the referrer's reward as though it were another payment to you.

## Match the connection to the household

Price is only one part of value. Write down the job the connection must do: video meetings, uploads, large downloads, streaming, or several of those at once.

When assessing an offer, ask about upload as well as download speed, the quoted service at your address, equipment costs, and any static-IP or remote-access requirement you actually have. A Wi-Fi coverage problem and an insufficient internet package are different problems; test before buying an upgrade to solve the wrong one.
