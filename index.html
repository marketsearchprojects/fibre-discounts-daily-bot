Two numbers. One clearer decision.

Keep the money you commit to paying separate from the benefit you might receive later. Conditional cashback is not a guaranteed reduction in your monthly bill.

Gross costAll broadband bills + required extras + setup charges + switching costs.

Conditional net costGross cost − eligible customer reward − separately confirmed, combinable reimbursements.

If a price changes during the contract, add each pricing stage separately. Count an exit-fee reimbursement only against an expense you also included. Subtracting a reimbursement without including the original fee invents a saving.

A monthly equivalent is a planning number, not your monthly invoice. It also says nothing about when a reward will arrive.

Why a cheaper headline can lose.

The starting example is fictional, not a live offer from any provider. Over 24 months, Offer A's £28 monthly broadband plus a required £3 extra costs £744. Offer B's £30 price with no required extra costs £720.

Without cashback, A costs £24 more. If A's £50 reward is received, its net cost becomes £694: £26 less than B. That is the decision to consider, alongside service suitability and the reward conditions—not simply which monthly headline looks smaller.

Always compare the full minimum commitment. A first-year calculation is useful for budgeting but does not show the whole liability on a longer contract. When terms differ, show both the contractual commitment and a common comparison horizon, identifying any assumed renewal prices.

Before you count a reward.

Save your address-specific quote, every monthly pricing stage, required extras, minimum term, and the order's referral confirmation. Record the customer reward separately from any reward paid to the referrer.

As a provider-specific example, YouFibre's published terms require the code on the original order and describe a qualifying 24-month contract, a new customer, service activation for at least 30 days and payment of the first full bill. The terms also restrict combining the referral scheme with other promotions. Confirm the current terms and your actual order before treating a reward as likely.

Match the connection to the job.

A small saving is not good value if the connection does not fit your household. Consider upload speed as well as download speed, the quoted service at your address, and any equipment or remote-access requirement you actually have. Diagnose a Wi-Fi coverage problem before paying for a faster internet package to solve the wrong issue.
